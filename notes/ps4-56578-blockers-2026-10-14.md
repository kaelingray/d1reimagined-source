## Blocker 2: teardown crash at +0xBC09AE

When the activity ends (inactivity timeout or phase 7) with a biped present, `c_simulation_sobject_entity_definition::vfunc[19]` (0xBC0990) dereferences a null 4th argument (an entity record). Without a biped (the team-0 run before the fix) the return to orbit is clean. So our biped lacks a replication entity record a real spawn would create. It may share a cause with blocker 1, since a fully registered player entity is what equipment would attach to. Next: find who calls vfunc 19 and where the record comes from (the entity record table next to `c_replication_entity_database_view`).

- An exec breakpoint on `c_action_impl_weapon_switch[2]` (0x39A7B0) hit once at spawn. The stack is the generic action tree: 0x36D400/0x36D220 → 0x3BC5F0/0x3BC4C0 → 0x36EB80 → 0x36EA40 → 0x36E8E0 → switch. Its feasibility check 0x3B4A60 (called from 0x3BBF50) runs once, and the action ends through [3].
- So "ready a weapon" is part of the default spawn behaviour. It fails because the biped has no weapon object. This confirms the symptom; it does not find the creator.

**Where blocker 1 stands.** Every client-side step we can see is done or running:
- loadout descriptors, ×3;
- weapon gear handles cached;
- gear resident (B+0x298 = 1);
- a ready-weapon action attempted.

The one missing event is the creation of the three weapon sobjects. No client code reachable from the spawn, player, gear, peer-view or investment paths attempts it. That points to creation being driven from outside the local spawn: a simulation-queue element or replicated entity from the authority. The best remaining candidates:
- **(a)** the entity/authority layer (udt 23/27/29 `claim_authority`, `reset/query_entity_slot_authority_mask`) that we never send;
- **(b)** a sim-queue element (`entity_handoff` / `entity_claim_authority`) that grants the owner the right to create its equipment entities.

Next: count the handlers for udt 23–32 and the sim-queue entity-authority definitions over a spawn, and log any udt-19 `allocate_entity_indices` requests the client sends after spawn. A request with no answer would be the smoking gun.

### Game engine and the launch goal (runs `gecov`, `mmgoal`)
- `c_game_engine` (vtable 0x194B430, a statically initialised global with no code reference to its vtable): slots 1 (0x9088F0), 5 (0x908B10) and 6 (0x9093E0) **never run**, at orbit or in world. The per-frame slots (0x907140, 0x907220, 0x907D40, 0x907ED0, 0x908220) do run. If a game-engine hook hands out the spawn loadout (as in Halo-lineage engines), it is one of the dead ones.
- Goals are world-controller rung numbers (`d1-eboot ladder`): 0x1B = 27 orbit, 0x1D = 29 matchmaking, 0x24 = 36 in_world.
- In `setup:matchmaking`, 0x7AB5D0 needs **session 0's goal = 0x24**; anything else gives "Fireteam is returning to orbit because the host changed the goal-state" → cleanup. It then reads **session 1's goal** (the activity session):
  - 0x1D: stay and keep matchmaking (the team pick 0x7AB270 runs from handler [7] once the matchmaking manager reaches state 3);
  - 0x24: "Activity-session host has finished matchmaking" → `setup:activity_host_setup`.
- Our client hosts session 1 and **mirrors session 0's goal into it within a second**. Writing 0x1D into session 1 the instant it appears was overwritten back to 0x24, and the client moved on as before. Goal pokes alone cannot get the client to matchmake. That needs the real Director launch: the matchmaking manager driven to "done" (state 3) while the activity session's goal is still 29.
- `d1-winguest.py`: `goal --session N [--value V --confirm]` and `launch --goal V`. `session()` takes a session index (stride 0x131C8).
